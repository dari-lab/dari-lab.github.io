I am an Associate Professor of Tropical Medicine in the Department of
Environmental Medical Biology at Hanyang University College of Medicine in
Seoul, South Korea. My research and teaching focus on demonstrating and
evaluating the effectiveness of health policy implementation through the
collection of primary data, with an emphasis on infectious disease surveillance
and global maternal and child health in the Asia-Pacific and beyond.

My research centers on generating and evaluating the evidence base for health
policy implementation. Through the collection and analysis of primary data—from
community-based household surveys to serological and molecular surveillance—I
assess whether policies and programs are implemented as intended and measure
their real-world effectiveness. Drawing on three years of field experience in
Fiji and doctoral work on lymphatic filariasis transmission assessment surveys
across Oceania, I currently lead a multi-country global health portfolio
spanning Laos, the Solomon Islands, Tanzania, Cambodia, and Fiji, in
partnership with KOFIH, KOICA, and the WHO, and I hold concurrent advisory
roles with the WHO. My work has been published in *Emerging Infectious
Diseases*, *The American Journal of Tropical Medicine and Hygiene*, and *PLoS
Neglected Tropical Diseases*, among others.

I received my M.D. from Ewha Womans University, a Diploma in Tropical Medicine
& Hygiene (DTM&H) from Mahidol University, an MPH from Harvard University, and a
Ph.D. from the University of Liverpool.
